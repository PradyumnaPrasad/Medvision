## Reading this honestly

- The current default (**0.30**) sits at the top of a smooth, sensible region
  (0.10–0.30): as the threshold rises through here, both recall and false-alarm
  rate fall together in an expected way, and 0.30 gives the best margin in that
  range.
- Beyond 0.35 the curve becomes **noisy and non-monotonic** — false-alarm rate
  spikes to 76.6% at 0.55 then drops to 34.2% at 0.70. That's not a smooth
  trade-off curve; it looks like individual findings' probability distributions
  clustering at specific values, not a real underlying trend. With only 304
  negative (normal) cases in this eval set, a single-point read like "0.70 is
  best" is not trustworthy — it could easily be sampling noise.
- **We did not change the default** based on this. The 0.70 point also only
  reflects one disease (Pneumonia); the graph has 15 other diseases we have no
  ground truth for, and tuning the *global* activation threshold to what's
  locally best for Pneumonia risks hurting recall for everything else (e.g.
  Tuberculosis, which shares several of the same finding nodes). Changing a
  shared, global parameter on single-disease evidence would be an overfit,
  not a fix.

## What would justify actually changing it

Per-disease ground truth for more of the 16 diseases in the knowledge graph
(PLAN.md T31/T12), so the threshold (or a per-disease threshold) could be chosen
against overall behavior instead of one disease's curve.
